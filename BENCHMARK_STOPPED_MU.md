# Stopped negative muons in Geant4: a benchmark against measurement

This document compares the secondaries that unpatched Geant4 11.4.2 and 11.5.0.beta create when the nucleus of a single-element target captures a stopped negative muon with yields measured for the same elements. It is generated by `scripts/generate_stopped_mu.py` from the files of `data/g4/stopped_mu/`, and every number it states is read from those files. Its section on capture products holds the comparison; the application that made the outputs is in `cpp/stopped_muon/`.

## What is run

Each event starts with a negative muon of zero kinetic energy at the centre of the box, a cube of 1 m half-side filled with a single element at a density of 1 g cm⁻³, and Geant4 transports it with the QBBC physics list on 1 thread. The event with global number i is seeded with 8675309 + i. Each cell holds the earliest 100000 nuclear captures of a target, configuration and revision in event order, and every event of a cell must be a single nuclear capture or a single decay in orbit, apart from the events without an outcome that the rule's post-data addition treats (below).

The configurations are reached as follows.

- `bertini`: the `bound_decay` route, what an unmodified QBBC application receives: `G4MuonMinusCapture` hands the capture to the Bertini cascade (`G4CascadeInterface`).
- `bertini_pc`: the same route with `G4CASCADE_USE_PRECOMPOUND=1`, which switches on the precompound de-excitation of the Bertini cascade.
- `precompound`: the `muonic_atom_helper` route, `G4MuonicAtomDecay` handing the capture to `G4MuMinusCapturePrecompound`, which an application receives only by registering constructors: QBBC's stopping constructor replaced by a `G4StoppingPhysics` whose last argument switches the capture at rest of a negative muon off in that constructor, a constructor that adds `G4MuonMinusAtomicCapture` as the rest process of the negative muon, and `G4MuonicAtomDecayPhysics`. Its rows describe what an application receives after those registrations, not what QBBC gives by default.

This opt-in by constructor registration is not the dataset opt-in. The harness option `--opt-in` switches `G4HadronicParameters::SetEnableMuonicData`, which exists only in a build that carries the patches of `cpp/patches/`; an unpatched build refuses `--opt-in on`, every cell runs with `--opt-in off`, and no row scores patched behaviour.

The cells run 17 targets: 11 of natural composition, each the element `G4NistManager::FindOrBuildElement` builds (natSi, natTi, natCa, natFe, natAg, natAu, natPb, natCu, natMo, natW and natI), and 6 single isotopes built from their Z and A (Al-27, Si-28, Ti-48, Cu-63, Pb-208 and C-12). ## The rule and when it was fixed

Each row compares the model's x, with its sampling standard error x_se, with a measured reference ref of statistical and systematic uncertainty ref_stat and ref_syst, through σ_tot, the square root of the sum of the squares of ref_stat, ref_syst and x_se. A row is PASS when |x − ref| ≤ 2 σ_tot. It is FAIL when |x − ref| > 3 σ_tot and, for a neutron-mean row, x also lies outside its rescue bracket [L, U]. Every other row is UNQUALIFIED: in tension when |x − ref| ≤ 3 σ_tot, efficiency-limited when it lies beyond that but inside the bracket. Each band includes its edge, and every decision is made on exact rationals parsed from the decimal strings.

The rescue bracket [L, U] of a neutron-mean row is the union of its 3 σ bands over r = ε₀/ε, with ε₀ = 0.545 and a detection efficiency ε anywhere in [0.40, 0.65], a design range fixed with the rule: r runs from r_lo = 0.8384 to r_hi = 1.3625, L is the lower edge of the band about r_lo ref, with ref_stat and ref_syst scaled by r_lo, and U the upper edge of the band about r_hi ref, scaled by r_hi, L rounded down and U up at 1e-12; `rule.rescue_edges` of the pre-registration states them in full. A missing row, an extra row, a reference cell that differs from the pre-registration or a rescue edge that differs from its recomputation is refused, never judged.

The pre-registration was fixed in parts, each before the data it governs unless it is marked as fixed after data:

| part | fixed (UTC) | relation to the data | contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| rule | 2026-10-13T14:21:38Z | fixed before any Geant4 run of the benchmark | the setup, the anchors, the rule, and the 25 predicates with their 150 rows |
| addition 1 | 2026-10-13T15:07:41Z | fixed before any Geant4 run of the benchmark | the f2 statistic on the full multiplicity, the gamma-line tag in the residual's rest frame, the per-line guards, the dataset binding and the 17 targets that are run |
| addition 2 | 2026-10-14T00:04:20Z | fixed before any Geant4 run of the benchmark | the f2.Pb and f2.Ag predicates and their 12 rows, from Table IV of MacDonald et al. |
| addition 3 | 2026-10-14T01:00:16Z | **fixed after data: after chunk 0 of all 102 cells, after 22 accepted cells of 11.4.2 and after the cell 11.4.2 bertini_pc natPb had been refused for its event 85953, whose capture step returned neither nuclear-capture nor decay products** | the treatment of events without an outcome and its rate guard (no_outcome) |

The addition fixed after data carries this correction: the rate guard is a limit on how often such events may occur, not a guarantee that excluding them changes no verdict: counting such an event as a capture with no products moves it into the earliest 100000 captures and moves the last capture out, which can shift a quantity by more than the rate alone suggests; whether a verdict changes is measured by re-running the rule under that alternative treatment.

What had been seen before a part was fixed:

- rule: seen before it was fixed, a preliminary Geant4 11.4.2 run of aluminium-27, lead-208 and silver-107 targets: aluminium proton, gamma-line and neutron yields and the lead-208 and silver-107 neutron means; the windows, k and the efficiency bracket were chosen from the sources and the study design, not from these numbers.
- addition 2: seen before it was fixed, the lead-208 neutron multiplicity of the preliminary run (its f2) and the silver-107 neutron mean; the statistic, its support and the uncertainty model were fixed before the source was read; only the transcription and the arithmetic are new.

**Neutrons.** A neutron-mean row is FAIL only when its x lies beyond 3 σ_tot of the mean at the published efficiency and outside the whole rescue bracket; 7 of the 21 neutron-mean rows of the table are. The f2 rows compare the second factorial moment of the multiplicity divided by the square of its mean, a statistic that a constant per-neutron detection efficiency leaves unchanged, with the same statistic of the measured distributions. The table gives each row's x, reference and bracket; no ratio of x to the reference is formed here.

**Events without an outcome.** These cells hold events whose capture step returned neither nuclear-capture products nor a decay:

- 11.4.2 `bertini_pc` natPb: 1 of 105968 stops (event 85953).
- 11.5.0.beta `bertini_pc` natPb: 1 of 105968 stops (event 85953).

The rule excludes such an event from both denominators and refuses a cell in which more than 0.001 of the stops have no outcome. That treatment is the pre-registration's addition 3 (2026-10-14T01:00:16Z), **fixed after data**: after chunk 0 of all 102 cells, after 22 accepted cells of 11.4.2 and after the cell 11.4.2 bertini_pc natPb had been refused for its event 85953, whose capture step returned neither nuclear-capture nor decay products. Counted instead as a capture with no products, the alternative treatment, each such event enters the earliest captures and moves the last capture out; the rule re-run on the alternative values of the 4 rows of these cells changes 0 of their verdicts.

**Chunk bodies.** Of the 102 pairs of chunks the revisions ran with the same seeds, 94 have identical compact bodies (the compact text without its header line) and 8 differ; every row value is nevertheless identical between the revisions.

**Shared seeds.** Every cell seeds the event with global number i with 8675309 + i, so the cells of a target, across configurations and revisions, are not independent samples: they are never pooled, and rows that agree between the revisions are not replications.

**Prior work and what these comparisons add.** Krikler compared the proton spectrum Geant4 10.1 gives for muon capture in aluminium with preliminary AlCap data (proceedings of NuFact15, pp. 408–417, <https://inspirehep.net/literature/1693740>). Blackwell compared the mean number of neutrons per capture that Geant4 9.6.p02 with the Shielding 2.0 physics list gives for negative muons stopped in aluminium, silicon, calcium, iron, silver, gold and lead, counting only the neutrons the capture produces directly, with the measured means that the neutron-mean rows here also use, and reported a systematic underestimate for these elements (doctoral thesis, University of Sheffield (2015), pp. 74–76, <https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/10432/1/Tim_Blackwell_PhD_Thesis.pdf>). Gaponenko et al. compared Geant4 10.2.p03 with their proton and deuteron spectra from aluminium, showing the prediction of the precompound model and stating that the default models of muon capture agree poorly with their data (Phys. Rev. C 101, 035502 (2020), arXiv:1908.06902). Kneißl reported a difference between simulated and measured rates of neutron signals that follow muon captures in lead, with Geant4 10.3, and limited its interpretation by the response of the detectors (doctoral thesis, Technische Universität München (2019), <https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1475093/document.pdf>). The LArIAT collaboration compared the residual nuclei of muon capture at rest in argon with Geant4 10.3.p03 (Phys. Rev. Lett. 134, 131801 (2025), arXiv:2408.05133).

Beside that work, the rows here compare, on Geant4 11.4.2 and 11.5.0.beta and in the configurations `bertini`, `bertini_pc` and `precompound`, the final AlCap yields with their uncertainties for protons, deuterons, tritons and alphas from aluminium, silicon and titanium; the TWIST integrals on every configuration; the γ lines of 26Mg per nuclear capture against Measday et al.; the mean neutron number per capture against the same measured means, judged within the efficiency bracket; and the f2 statistic against MacDonald et al., under a rule fixed before the runs apart from the addition the section above marks as fixed after data.

**Limits.** QBBC only; the capture step only (reactions of the secondaries and later decays are not counted); a single element of the stated density in a cube of the stated half-side, natural targets with the composition `G4NistManager` builds; the neutron means are MacDonald et al.'s as Singer tabulates them, read in Blackwell's thesis; the γ-line references are the yields Measday et al. give per capture, normalised through their capture probability, which the ENSDF evaluation repeats; the f2 references for aluminium and silicon come from an evaluated distribution that assumes no capture emits more than 3 neutrons, while the model's f2 keeps every multiplicity; the f2 references for lead and silver are those of the detected distributions at efficiency 0.545, equal to the true f2 only if every neutron is detected with the same probability independently of the others; the efficiency bracket is a fixed design range, not an uncertainty a source states; this benchmark does not establish the mechanism behind any disagreement.

## Reproducing

`cpp/stopped_muon/README.md` describes the application and its driver. A cell is rebuilt and re-run against an unpatched Geant4 install and its datasets with:

```sh
python3 cpp/stopped_muon/stopped_muon.py build --install <prefix> --build <dir>
python3 cpp/stopped_muon/stopped_muon.py reproduce --cell <version>/<config>/<target> --binary <exe> --install <prefix> --data <dir> --work <dir> --out <file>
```

The cells 11.4.2 bertini Al-27, 11.5.0.beta precompound natSi and 11.4.2 bertini_pc natPb were re-run chunk by chunk with the application rebuilt from the committed sources (`data/g4/stopped_mu/reproduced.csv`): each of their 6 chunks gave compact text with the sha256 the committed cell records, so the re-run cells reduce to the committed records.
